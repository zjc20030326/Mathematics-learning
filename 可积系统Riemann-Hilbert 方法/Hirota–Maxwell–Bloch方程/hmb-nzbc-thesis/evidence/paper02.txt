1. Introduction


   Since the inverse scattering transform (IST) was ﬁrst presented to solve exactly the well-known Korteweg-de Vries (KdV)
equation with a Lax pair [1] , the IST has been used to study more and more physically important nonlinear wave equations
with Lax pairs such as the nonlinear Schrödinger (NLS) equation, modiﬁed KdV equation, Benjamin-Ono equation, sine-
Gordon equation, Kadomtsev-Petviashvili equation, Davey-Stewartson equation, and etc. (see, e.g., Refs. [2–4] and references
therein). As physically interesting models, the NLS equation and its extensions appear in many ﬁelds of nonlinear science
such as nonlinear optics, Bose-Einstein condensates, deep ocean, and even ﬁnance (see, e.g., Refs. [5–14] and references
therein). The focusing ( σ = −1 ) and defocusing ( σ = 1 ) NLS equations
       iqt + q xx −2 σ (| q | 2 −q 20 ) q = 0 ,  σ = ±1 ,  q 0 ∈ R                                                                (1)
with ZBC, lim x →±∞ q (x, t) = 0 and q 0 = 0 , and NZBCs, limx →±∞ q (x, t) = q ± with | q ±| = q 0 = 0 , have been solved via the
standard IST and its extension, bilinear method, and dress method (see, e.g., Refs. [8,15–30] and references therein). Recently,
the ISTs of integrable nonlinear wave equations with NZBCs have been paid much attention to based on the solutions of the
related Riemann-Hilbert problems (see, e.g., Refs. [22–30] and references therein).

   To study more complicated nonlinear wave phenomena such as the phenomena of oceanic and optical waves, the exten-
sions of the NLS equation were needed. For example, when the optical pulses become shorter (e.g., 100 fs [6] ), higher-order
dispersive and nonlinear effects such as third-order dispersion, self-frequency shift, and self-steepening arising from the
stimulated Raman scattering are signiﬁcant in the study of ultra-short optical pulse propagation [31–33] . The Hirota equa-
tion [34] is an important one of the above-mentioned higher-order NLS equations, and is completely integrable.
   This paper focuses on the ISTs and solutions for both focusing and defocusing Hirota equations [34] with NZBCs at inﬁnity

4. Conclusions and discussions


   In conclusion, we have presented the ISTs for both focusing and defocusing Hirota equations with NZBCs at inﬁnity. Based
on the matrix Riemann-Hilbert problems and different types of poles, we have obtained the solutions of the focusing and
defocusing Hirota equations with NZBCs. Particularly, we give the explicit expressions for the reﬂectionless potentials in both
focusing and defocusing cases. Moreover, we exhibit some simple-pole and double-pole solutions for the focusing case, and
the simple-pole solutions for the defocusing case. These results can also reduce to ones of the both focusing and defocusing
NLS equations and complex mKdV equations with NZBCs. Of course, the idea used in this paper can also be extended to
study the ISTs and solutions for the focusing and defocusing mKdV equation [43] , nonlocal mKV equation [44] , derivative
NLS equation [45] , higher-order NLS equation [37] , or other nonlocal NLS equations [14,46,47] with NZBCs.
