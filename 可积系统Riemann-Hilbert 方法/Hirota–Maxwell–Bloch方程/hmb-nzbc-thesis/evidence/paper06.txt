             equation via the Riemann–Hilbert approach

                                  Published 19 June 2021

     In this paper, we study the N-soliton solutions for the Hirota and Maxwell–Bloch equa-
     tion with physical meaning. From the Lax pair and Volterra integral equations, the
    Riemann–Hilbert problem of this integrable equation is constructed. By solving the
    matrix Riemann–Hilbert problem with the condition of no reﬂecting, the N-soliton solu-
     tions for the Hirota and Maxwell–Bloch equation are obtained explicitly. Finally, we
     simulate the three-dimensional diagram of |E| with 2-soliton solutions and the motion
     trajectory of t-axis in the case of diﬀerent z.

    Keywords: Hirota and Maxwell–Bloch equation; Riemann–Hilbert problem; N-soliton
     solutions; Lax pair; integrable equation.

   PACS number: 05.45.Yv


1. Introduction

In this paper, we consider the integrable Hirota and Maxwell–Bloch (HMB) equa-
tion with the zero boundary condition at inﬁnity1

where A1, A2, A3, ω are real constants, ∗represents the complex conjugate, E is
the ﬁeld, p is the polarization and η is the population inversion. In fact, the HMB
equation has several applications about higher-order dispersion, nonlinear eﬀect,
self-steepening, nonlinear ﬁber optics in physics, and the soliton solutions to the
HMB equation are also obtained by Darboux transformation.2 In fact, the soliton
solutions of the HMB equation can be constructed by the matrix Riemann–Hilbert
problem which was born out of inverse scattering transformation in 1997.3 A few
years later, people applied it to solve integrable equations, for example, the modiﬁed
Korteweg–de Vries equation,4 the multi-component AKNS integrable hierarchies,5
the nonlocal Fokas–Lenells equation,6 the Kundu-nonlinear Schr¨odinger equation7
the N-coupled Hirota equations,8 the coupled modiﬁed nonlinear Schr¨odinger
equations,9 the coupled nonlinear Schr¨odinger equations,10 the coupled derivative
Schr¨odinger equation,11 the Kundu-Type equation12 and the generalized Sasa–
Satsuma equation13,14 so on. Furthermore, the Riemann–Hilbert method can not
only be applied to construct the N-soliton solutions, but also be used for the initial-
boundary value problems and the long-time asymptotic behaviors of defocusing
systems.15–22
   This paper is arranged as follows. In Sec. 2, from the Lax pair of the HMB
equation and the full derivative form (2.4), we obtain the asymptotic condition of φ.
By introducing two related Volterra integral equations and some deﬁnitions, we can
construct Riemann–Hilbert problem of the HMB equation. In Sec. 3, the N-soliton
solutions to the HMB equation are obtained in detail and some three-dimensional
images of N-soliton solutions are shown. In Sec. 4, we give a brief conclusion of this
paper and present some discussions in integrable systems.

4. Conclusion and Discussion

In this paper, we construct N-soliton solutions to the HMB equation via Riemann–
Hilbert method. In fact, the HMB equation is a kind of important equation in
light communication, which can be equivalent to nonlinear Schr¨odinger–Maxwell–
Bloch (NLS-MB) equation under a certain constraint, so it is still very important to
continue to study it. Furthermore, using Riemann–Hilbert method to solve nonlocal
integrable equations and integrable hierarchies is also a very meaningful work, which
not only provides a method to solve integrable equations but also promotes the
development of integrable systems. In the following work, we will focus on the long-
time asymptotics of integrable equations.
