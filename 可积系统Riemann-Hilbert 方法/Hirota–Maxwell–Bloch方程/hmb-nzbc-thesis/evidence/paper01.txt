1. Introduction

   The exact solution [1,2] serves as a fundamental cornerstone in the study of soliton and integrable system theory. Previous inves-
tigations have yielded remarkable insights into nonlinear waves of integrable equations, and the analysis of soliton solutions has long
been a core research focus in the ﬁeld of integrable systems. In recent years, by developing the Riemann-Hilbert method, researchers
successfully derived the N-soliton solutions for the general n-component nonlinear Schrödinger equations and proposed a conjecture
regarding the law of nonlinear wave propagation [3]. Meanwhile, the steepest descent method has been applied to investigate the
long-time asymptotic behavior of solutions to the non-local LPD equation with step-like initial data, yielding profound insights into
the evolutionary dynamics of such systems [4]. These advances not only enrich the theoretical framework of soliton theory but also
provide important methodological references for studying more complex multi-component integrable models. Although solitons and
breathers have been extensively studied against a constant background, it is widely recognized that such a scenario corresponds to

an idealized situation. Therefore, investigating solutions featuring periodic backgrounds has garnered signiﬁcant academic interest,
and research on breathers over periodic backgrounds has gained considerable momentum in recent years.
   In 2017, Rao and He contributed to this ﬁeld through their work on the nonlocal Davey-Stewartson equation [5], building upon
foundational studies by Chen and Pelinovsky [6–9]. By employing the Darboux transformation method and the nonlinearization of the
Lax pair [9–17], periodic rogue wave solutions have been derived for the modiﬁed Korteweg-deVries equation [8] and the nonlinear
Schrödinger equation [6,7]. Liu and Li adopted the KP hierarchy reduction method to obtain related solutions [18], while Liu, Rao,
and Qiao conducted further investigations on periodic breather solutions [19]. Shi and Zhaqilao explored breather solution on a
periodic background for the Kundu equation [20], and Xu and Yang carried out analogous studies for the Gross-Pitaevskii equation
[21]. Huang and Guo examined breathers over diﬀerent periodic backgrounds [22], and Chang and Zhaqilao investigated periodic
rogue wave solutions [23] and so on.
    It is well established that multi-component nonlinear equations generally exhibit rich and diverse wave solution structures. In this
context, we consider the Hirota and Maxwell-Bloch equations [24]

     𝑢𝑡= i𝛼(1 𝑢𝑥𝑥+ |𝑢|2𝑢) + 𝛽(𝑢𝑥𝑥𝑥+ 6|𝑢|2𝑢𝑥) + 2𝑝,                                                                           (1a)             2
     𝑝𝑥= 2i𝜔𝑝+ 2𝑢𝜂,                                                                                                    (1b)
     𝜂𝑥= −(𝑢𝑝∗+ 𝑢∗𝑝),                                                                                                       (1c)
where 𝑢, 𝑝 and 𝜂 are complex functions: 𝑢 denotes the normalized slowly varying amplitude of the complex ﬁeld envelope, 𝑝 represents
polarization, 𝜂 stands for population inversion, and 𝛼, 𝛽 and 𝜔 are real constants. Here, ∗ indicates complex conjugation. For the Hirota
and Maxwell-Bloch equations, reference [23] addressed soliton solutions, rogue waves, and the N-fold Darboux transformation. The
present work aims to extend this line of research by investigating periodic breathers and breathers with periodic structure in the
multi-component Hirota and Maxwell-Bloch equations.
   The structure of this paper is organized as follows. In Section 2, the Lax pair and the Darboux transformation are introduced.
Section 3 presents the derivation of traveling periodic solutions. In Section 4, a general solution to the Lax pair given by Eqs. (2) and
(3) is constructed. Three special cases are considered by employing the plane wave, cn-periodic wave, and dn-periodic wave as seed
solutions of Equation (1), leading to three corresponding general solutions. In Section 5, the one-fold Darboux transformation and the
two-fold Darboux transformation are applied to obtain various types of nonlinear wave solutions for the Hirota and Maxwell-Bloch
equations, including breathers on the soliton background, two-soliton, one-breather and two-breather on the periodic background, as
well as breathers and breathers with periodic structure on the cn-periodic wave background, along with breathers and anti-breathers
on the dn-periodic wave background. In Section 6, by combining the Darboux transformation with the nonlinearization of the Lax
pair, rogue waves on the cn- and dn-periodic wave backgrounds of Jacobian elliptic functions are obtained. Section 7 summarizes
the key ﬁndings and provides concluding remarks.

7. Conclusion

        In the paper, we have investigated breathers on the soliton backgrounds, two-soliton, one-breather and two-breather on the
periodic backgrounds by analyzing three special cases derived from a general solution of Equation (1). Compared to existing studies,
breathers on the periodic backgrounds in multi-component systems remain relatively underexplored. For the cn-periodic case, a
decrease in the elliptic modulus 𝑚 leads to a transition from breathers on the cn-periodic background to breathers with periodic
structure on the same background. When 𝜌 is real, breathers and anti-breathers on the dn-periodic background can be obtained.
Regarding propagation characteristics: for breathers on the periodic background, the background wave propagates along the t-axis,
while the breather propagates along either the x-axis or the t-axis. In contrast, for breathers on cn- and dn-periodic backgrounds, the
breather travels along the t-axis, while the background wave propagates along the x-axis. By combining the Darboux transformation
with the nonlinearization of the Lax pair, rogue waves on the cn- and dn-periodic backgrounds of Jacobian elliptic functions are
obtained. Speciﬁcally, the rogue waves on the cn-periodic background are linear, and the periodic background wave amplitudes of
the three potentials exhibit a consistent regularity. Table 1 clearly illustrates the existence conditions of diﬀerent types of solutions
in the parameter space. These results contribute meaningful insights to the exploration of other integrable systems. It is hoped that
the periodic wave solutions presented in this study will inspire new directions in future research.
